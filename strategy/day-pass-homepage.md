# The $40 Day — a marketing pass on the homepage

*Reimagining the Primal Moves Venice homepage around the day pass, in a warm
third-place register.*

---

## The shift in one line

We stop asking people to **commit to a practice** and start inviting them to
**spend a day somewhere good.**

The two-week trial asks: *are you ready to change your life?* That's a big
question to put to a stranger. The day pass asks: *what are you doing Saturday?*
One of those is easy to say yes to.

---

## The unlock

**"A Day at Primal" was already the best section on the site. Now it's the
product.**

We built that timeline as a storytelling device — a way to help someone picture
inhabiting the space. With the day pass as the hero offer, the story and the
thing being sold become the same object. The homepage stops describing a
practice and starts describing *a day you could have*, with a price on it.

That's rare. Most of the time you have to invent the connection between your
best content and your offer. Here it already exists.

**Everything follows from this:** the timeline moves up, gains a price, and
becomes the spine of the page.

## The positioning

> **The third place.** Not home, not work — the other one.

"Where everybody knows your name" is the feeling, and it's worth being precise
about why it fits: we now include **cowork**. That means the building can hold
your whole day. Home, work, here. That's the definition of a third place, and
almost no gym can claim it because almost no gym gives you anywhere to sit down
and be a person.

This also solves the brief's hardest problem. It has been asking "what is
Primal?" and finding the answer slippery because Primal means different things
to different people. **"Your third place" absorbs that instead of fighting it.**
Everyone's third place is different — that's what makes it theirs.

---

## Rename the offer

"Day Pass" is the wrong name for what this is. A day pass sounds like a guest
pass at a gym — something you tolerate until you join.

What's actually being sold is a **whole day of a wellness club**: a class, a
sauna, a cold plunge, coffee, a desk, tea, quiet. Call it what it is.

Candidates, warmest first:

- **the $40 day** — plain, concrete, does the work
- **a day at Primal** — ties the offer to the section it belongs to
- **the open day** — invitational, slightly literary
- **one day, all of it** — a bit more sell

My pick: **"a day at Primal — $40"**. The offer, the story and the section all
carry the same name, so the page only has to teach one idea.

## The value argument, using your own numbers

Don't reach for competitor pricing. You already have the proof internally:

> Your own members pay **$25–30 for a single extra class.**

So $40 buys the class — at roughly member rates — and everything else comes
free: sauna, cold plunge, gym, coffee, a desk, tea. Stated plainly, without a
crossed-out fake price, that's a genuinely strong argument. Say it once, quietly,
and let people do the arithmetic themselves. Warm brands don't shout about value.

## Homepage, reimagined

### 1 · Hero — an invitation, not a command

The current headlines are a command ("move like you mean it") and a description
("a daily movement practice"). Neither is warm. An invitation is:

> # come for the day
> **$40.** A class, the sauna, the cold plunge, coffee, a desk if you need one,
> and tea when you're done. Stay as long as you like.
>
> `[ book a day → ]`   `try primal online free`

Note what's missing: no urgency, no "transform", no countdown. The tone is a
friend telling you about somewhere good.

### 2 · What forty dollars gets you

The heart of the page. The Day at Primal timeline, but now it's the menu — and
the point is that **all of it is one price**.

| | |
|---|---|
| 7:00 | **restore** — start softly, a class that brings you into your body |
| 8:45 | **compound** — or lift something heavy instead, if that's your mood |
| 10:00 | **primal** — the signature practice, where everyone starts |
| 11:00 | **nourish** — coffee and breakfast at Cherish. Sit down. Stay. |
| all day | **work** — take a seat, use the wifi, nobody's counting your hours |
| afternoon | **sauna + plunge** — hot, then cold, then very pleased with yourself |
| 5:30 | **progressions** — get upside down, change your perspective |
| 7:00 | **gather** — a workshop, dinner, music, whatever's on |

Closing line, doing the value argument once:

> *That's one price. Our members pay $25–30 for the class alone.*

### 3 · You don't have to do all of it

**The anti-intimidation section**, and possibly the most important on the page.
The eight-item list above is thrilling to some people and exhausting to others.
Immediately give permission to do almost nothing:

> Plenty of people buy a day pass, sit in the sauna, drink a coffee and go home.
> That's a completely respectable use of forty dollars. Nobody's keeping score.

That paragraph will convert people the rest of the page scares off.

### 4 · Where everybody knows your name

The community section — but shown, not claimed. Photographs of people *between*
things: talking, laughing, drinking coffee, sitting on the floor with their
shoes off. Short member quotes when you have them.

> It's not that the workout is different. It's that you know six people in the
> room, and one of them saved you a spot.

### 5 · The rooms

Room-by-room, warm and factual — main floor, sauna, cold plunge, lounge, tea
room, Cherish. Less "amenities," more "here's where you'd actually be."

### 6 · Cherish

Unchanged in spirit, but it earns more space now, because coffee and a place to
sit are part of the offer rather than a nice extra.

### 7 · Coming back

Where membership finally appears — as the natural consequence of liking it,
never as the ask:

> Come often enough and a membership is cheaper. That's the only reason to get
> one. `[ see membership options → ]`

### 8 · Book a day

Repeat the offer. One button. No countdown, no "limited spots."

## What moves off the homepage

- **The two-week trial** → a card in the membership list, framed as the step
  after a good day, not the front door.
- **Every membership tier** → membership page, as now.
- **The four-series method** → the Practice page. Explaining the method up front
  is exactly the intimidating move we're trying to avoid; people don't need a
  curriculum to book a Saturday.

---

## Photography — the hard note

**Your best photograph is working against this positioning.**

The wall of handstands is the most arresting image in the library. It is also
the *least welcoming* one — it says "everyone here can do something you can't."
For a page whose whole job is "you'd be comfortable here," that's the wrong
first impression.

For this direction the hero wants: people mid-laugh, the sauna, coffee in hands,
a room of ordinary bodies working. You already own several — `joy-laughing`,
`sauna-laughing`, `cherish` interiors, `rest-floor`.

Keep the handstand wall. Move it to the Practice page, where aspiration belongs.

**Shots you don't have and would need:** someone working on a laptop in the
lounge, the coffee itself, tea being poured, two people talking after class,
the cold plunge with someone actually laughing in it.

---

## Tone rules

**Do:** plain words, short sentences, specific detail ("someone saved you a
spot" beats "community"), gentle humour, permission to do less.

**Don't:** transform, unlock, journey, elevate, crush, "your best self,"
countdowns, fake scarcity, before-and-afters, anything a supplement ad would say.

**The test:** would you say this sentence to a nervous friend who thinks they're
not fit enough? If not, cut it.

---

## Two things to settle first

**1. Does the $40 day pass actually include all of this?** Per your Mindbody
listing, the day pass is *one class plus sauna, cold plunge and gym access*.
Coffee, cowork and tea ceremony aren't in it — tea ceremony is a paid extra or a
membership perk. So this is a **product change before it's a marketing change.**
If we publish the list above, the front desk has to honour it.

**2. What does "coffee" mean?** One drip coffee included, or anything at
Cherish? "Coffee included" and "a $12 matcha included" are very different
businesses. Worth deciding before it's on the homepage.

## Two edits to the original, and why

**"The only place you can…"** — a superlative that's hard to defend. Several LA
clubs pair a sauna with a cafe, and one competitor screenshot is all it takes to
make the line a liability. It also isn't the tone we're going for: the warm move
is to describe, not to claim primacy.

Keep the punch without the risk by using your own arithmetic instead — *"our
members pay $25–30 for the class alone"* — or by asking rather than asserting:
*"where else can you do all that for forty dollars?"* A question is inviting.
A superlative is a boast.

**"Book your first experience today."** Two small problems. "Experience" is
corporate-wellness language — it's the word a brand uses when it doesn't want to
say what the thing is. And "today" is urgency, which cuts against warm and
unintimidating.

**"Book your first day"** fixes both: plainer, and it reinforces that the unit
being sold is a whole day rather than a class.

*(Also: the original reads "take of your body and mind" — missing "care".)*

## The line worth protecting

> **with other people**

That phrase is the entire third-place argument compressed into three words, and
it's the one thing a home gym, an app and a Peloton cannot offer. If anything
gets cut for length, cut something else.
